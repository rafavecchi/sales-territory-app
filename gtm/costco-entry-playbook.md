# Costco Entry Playbook — Frozen Coxinhas (Utah / Mountain Region)

Costco is the beachhead. This is the operator's guide to getting in.

## How Costco actually buys (mental model)

- **SKU-scarce by design.** A warehouse carries ~3,800 SKUs (vs. 40,000+ at a supermarket). Every slot is precious, so buyers bet only on **proven velocity and clear value**. Your job is to remove their risk.
- **Regional vs. national buyers.** National buyers (Issaquah, WA HQ) handle nationwide items. **Regional buyers** handle local/regional items and **Road Shows** — this is your realistic door. Utah sits in Costco's **Mountain region**.
- **Markup cap.** Costco caps markup (~14–15%) and *wants* to. That's the shopper "wow" — and the reason your COGS discipline is everything.
- **Value math.** A Costco item must feel like an unmissable deal vs. buying the equivalent elsewhere. Big pack, low unit cost, high quality.

## The wedge: Costco Road Shows

A **Road Show** is a temporary (typically 1–2 week) in-warehouse event where your product is actively sold and sampled, usually staffed by a Road Show operator's team. Why it's the best entry for an unknown brand:

- It **generates real sell-through data** in Costco's own environment.
- Strong Road Show numbers are the classic path to a **permanent SKU** — many national Costco items began as Road Shows.
- It gets you **in front of the regional buyer** with proof, not promises.

**How to pursue one:**
1. Identify and pitch the **Costco Mountain-region buyer** for frozen foods.
2. Partner with an established **Road Show operator/distributor** (they have the relationships, staff, insurance, and logistics to run events). Start these conversations early — this is often the true gatekeeper.
3. Come prepared with samples, packaging, cost/value math, and any velocity data you've already gathered at markets/independents.

## Parallel track: RangeMe

Costco (and many buyers) accept submissions via **RangeMe**. Create a high-quality listing: professional pack shots, the air-fryer story, margins, certifications, capacity. It's a low-cost way to get discovered and to look legitimate.

## Supplier requirements checklist (build toward these)

- [ ] **USDA-inspected production** with establishment number on label (poultry = FSIS).
- [ ] **GFSI certification** at the facility (SQF or BRCGS) — usually your co-packer's.
- [ ] **Product & general liability insurance** (~$2–5M; retailer as additional insured).
- [ ] **Costco Supplier Code of Conduct** acknowledged.
- [ ] **EDI** capability for POs/invoices.
- [ ] **UPC/GTIN** barcodes; correct **case pack & pallet configuration**.
- [ ] **Frozen cold-chain** distribution (frozen distributor or ship-to-depot).
- [ ] **Capacity to scale** — proof you can supply warehouse-level volume without stockouts.
- [ ] **Nutrition + allergen labeling** (wheat/egg/milk), safe-handling statement, "fully cooked," air-fryer instructions.

## The buyer meeting — what to bring

1. **Velocity proof:** units/day from Road Shows, markets, and any stocked accounts.
2. **Value story:** your club retail price vs. comparable items; unit-cost advantage.
3. **Margin math:** confirms you work within their markup cap and stay healthy.
4. **Capacity proof:** co-packer scale, lead times, fill-rate commitment.
5. **The product, cooked:** sample it hot from an air fryer, with the dip.

## Sequencing with Sam's Club & BJ's

- **Sam's Club:** has supplier onboarding and local-supplier paths; run demo events in parallel. A second club creates urgency and a fallback.
- **BJ's:** East-Coast footprint — a later-phase target once you expand east.

## Do / Don't

**Do:** lead with velocity data • size the pack for club value • solve USDA + capacity first • use Road Shows as paid proof • target the regional buyer.
**Don't:** cold-pitch national HQ with a deck and no data • promise volume you can't supply • price where you can't survive the markup cap • rely on ethnic-nostalgia demand in Utah.
