# Go-To-Market Plan — Frozen Coxinhas (Air-Fryer Chicken Bites)

**Product:** A retail bag of 10–20 small coxinhas (breaded shredded-chicken bites). Consumer drops them in an air fryer, ~10–12 minutes, done.
**Starting market:** Utah (Wasatch Front)
**First channel:** Club stores — Costco (primary), Sam's Club / BJ's (secondary)
**Model:** Prove velocity locally → win a Costco regional/roadshow slot → expand region by region.

> **Working assumption on positioning:** In Utah the Brazilian-diaspora "nostalgia" demand is thin. Do **not** sell this as an ethnic specialty. Sell it as a **crispy chicken snack built for the air fryer** — a party/game-day/big-family appetizer that happens to be a coxinha. Keep the name and the story, but let the hero benefit be *"crispy chicken bites, air-fryer easy, kids and a crowd love them."* This single decision drives packaging, pricing, and sampling below.

---

## 1. Executive summary

The fastest realistic path into club stores is **not** a cold pitch to a national buyer. It's a three-step wedge:

1. **Prove the product sells itself** at in-person demo events (Costco Road Shows, Sam's Club supplier events, Utah farmers markets, Costco warehouse sampling). Club buyers buy *proven velocity*, not decks.
2. **Land a Costco Road Show / regional-buyer slot** in the Mountain region (Utah has ~10 Costco warehouses within an hour of SLC). Use the sell-through numbers as your proof.
3. **Scale region by region** — Mountain West → national — as manufacturing, food-safety certification, and working capital allow.

The two hard gates you must clear *before* any of that are: (a) **USDA-inspected production** (coxinhas are a poultry product — this is non-negotiable and is the #1 thing founders miss), and (b) **unit economics that survive Costco's ~14% markup cap** while still delivering a "wow" club value. Everything else is execution.

## 2. The product & positioning

| Element | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Hero benefit** | "Crispy chicken bites, air-fryer ready in ~12 minutes." Convenience + crowd-pleaser first; "authentic Brazilian coxinha" second. |
| **Occasions** | Game day, kids' after-school, party appetizer, potluck, quick protein snack. Utah families are large — lean into *"feeds a crowd."* |
| **Pack for club** | Club needs a big, high-value bag. Target **~2–2.5 lb / 24–30 count** resealable bag (bump the 10–20 count up for club economics), or a **2-bag club multipack**. Retail-grocery packs stay at 10–20 count. |
| **Dips angle** | Include or suggest a dipping sauce (ranch, spicy mayo, chimichurri, garlic). Dips lift perceived value and give a marketing hook. |
| **Naming** | Keep "coxinha" but pair it with a plain-English descriptor on-pack: e.g. *"Coxinhas — Brazilian Crispy Chicken Bites."* Shoppers must understand it in 2 seconds at a freezer door. |
| **Frozen, not par-baked** | Sell fully-cooked-then-frozen so the consumer only reheats. Simplifies food-safety messaging ("fully cooked") and cook instructions. |

**Competitive shelf context (club frozen appetizers):** Bibigo potstickers, empanadas, churros, mozzarella sticks, chicken bites, pork belly bites. These typically sit at **$9.99–$14.99** for a large bag. That band is your pricing gravity.

## 3. The two hard gates (clear these first)

### Gate A — USDA production (the one founders miss)
Coxinhas contain cooked chicken above the FSIS threshold, so the product falls under **USDA FSIS** jurisdiction, **not** FDA. Practically:

- You must produce in a **USDA-inspected facility** (your own with a grant of inspection, or — far more likely at this stage — a **USDA co-packer**).
- Your **label needs USDA compliance** (establishment number, safe-handling statement, correct product name, nutrition panel, allergens — wheat, egg, milk likely).
- A home kitchen or standard commercial kitchen **cannot** legally produce this for interstate/club retail.

**Action:** Line up a USDA-inspected co-packer that can handle breaded poultry appetizers *before* you pitch anyone. This is your critical-path constraint. (Utah/Mountain-West poultry & frozen co-packers exist; also consider co-packers in ID, CO, CA within freight range.)

### Gate B — Club-store supplier requirements
Costco/Sam's will require, roughly:
- **GFSI food-safety certification** at the production facility (SQF or BRCGS) — your co-packer should already hold this.
- **Product & general liability insurance** (commonly **$2–5M**), naming the retailer as additional insured.
- **Costco Supplier Code of Conduct** compliance, EDI capability, UPC/GTIN barcodes, and case/pallet configs that fit their logistics.
- A **cold chain** (frozen) distribution path — either DSD via a frozen distributor or ship-to-depot.

You don't need all of this to run a Road Show, but you need it to become a stocked item. Build toward it in parallel.

## 4. Unit economics (the make-or-break math)

Club stores cap markup low (**Costco ~14–15%**), which means your wholesale price and retail price are close — great for the shopper, brutal on a thin cost structure. Work the math backward from the shelf. See **`unit-economics.md`** for the editable model. Rough illustrative target:

| Line | Illustrative target (per club bag) |
|---|---|
| Club retail price | **$11.99** |
| Costco pays you (~÷1.14) | **~$10.50** |
| Freight + cold-chain to depot | your number |
| Co-pack COGS (chicken, breading, bag, labor) | must land so that **gross margin ≥ ~25–30%** after freight |
| Slotting / demo / spoilage reserve | budget separately |

**Rules of thumb:**
- If you can't hit a **healthy margin at ~$11.99 retail**, the club channel will not work — fix COGS (bigger production runs, cheaper cut, co-pack negotiation) before pitching.
- Club volume is enormous but unforgiving: a single warehouse can move hundreds of units/week. Make sure your co-packer can **scale supply** before you win, or a "yes" becomes a stockout that kills the relationship.

## 5. Channel strategy — landing Costco (primary)

Costco is the goal; here's the realistic funnel. Full detail in **`costco-entry-playbook.md`**.

1. **RangeMe + regional buyer.** Costco sources through **RangeMe**; create a strong listing. Simultaneously target the **Costco Mountain-region buyer** for frozen foods (regional buyers handle local items and Road Shows — a far more reachable door than national HQ in Issaquah).
2. **Costco Road Show = your wedge.** A Road Show is a 1–2 week in-warehouse event where you (or a Road Show operator) actively sell your product. It's the single best way for an unknown brand to *prove velocity* and get a buyer's attention. Many national Costco items started as Road Shows.
   - You often need a **Road Show operator/distributor partner** to run these; start those conversations early.
3. **Bring data, not a pitch.** After demos/Road Shows, walk into the buyer meeting with **units sold per day, sampling conversion, repeat interest, and margin math**. That's what converts.
4. **Sam's Club & BJ's in parallel.** Sam's has supplier onboarding and local-supplier paths; BJ's is East-Coast (later). Pitching two clubs creates urgency and a fallback.

**Why this sequence beats a cold national pitch:** club buyers are betting their warehouse's limited SKU slots on velocity. Give them proof, and de-risk the bet.

## 6. Demand proof before the club "yes"

You need velocity evidence and a small revenue base. Run these in Utah *now*, in parallel with co-packer setup:

- **Costco Road Shows / Sam's supplier demo events** — the direct wedge.
- **In-warehouse sampling** (Club Demonstration Services runs Costco sampling) once stocked.
- **Farmers markets & food festivals** (SLC Downtown Farmers Market, Real Food Rising, cultural festivals) — cheap, high-signal demand testing and content.
- **A few independent/specialty freezers & Utah grocers** (Harmons is a strong Utah regional that supports local brands; also Winco, Smith's) — a stepping-stone SKU at 10–20 count while you chase club.
- **Air-fryer content marketing** — short vertical videos of the crisp-up + dip. This is a made-for-social product; use it to build the brand and gather "people want this" proof.

Capture every event's lead/velocity data — this is your ammunition for the buyer meeting. (Your own **Nox canvassing app** can double as the field tool to log demo locations, sell-through, and retailer leads as you expand into independents and grocery.)

## 7. Phased rollout

**Phase 0 — Foundations (Months 0–3)**
Lock USDA co-packer • finalize recipe & shelf-stable frozen spec • club-format packaging + UPC • liability insurance • cost model validated at $11.99 • brand name/label • RangeMe listing.

**Phase 1 — Prove it in Utah (Months 2–6)**
Farmers markets + festivals • Harmons/independent Utah placement (10–20 ct) • apply for Costco Road Shows and Sam's demo events • collect velocity data • build social content library.

**Phase 2 — Win the club slot (Months 5–10)**
Run Costco Road Shows (Mountain region) • convert data into a regional-buyer meeting • secure a regional Costco/Sam's placement • stand up frozen distribution/cold chain • ensure co-packer can scale.

**Phase 3 — Expand region by region (Months 9–24)**
Add Costco regions (Mountain West → Southwest → national) • expand grocery (regional chains via broker) • add SKUs (flavors, dip-included, larger multipacks) • pursue national Costco / Kirkland conversation once velocity is proven at scale.

## 8. KPIs to run the business by

- **Road Show / demo velocity:** units sold per warehouse per day (the buyer's #1 metric).
- **Sampling conversion:** % of samplers who buy.
- **Reorder / repeat rate** at any stocked account.
- **Gross margin %** after freight (guard the $11.99 math).
- **Fill rate / stockout days** (a won account you can't supply is worse than no account).
- **Warehouses/accounts live** and **weekly velocity trend**.

## 9. Key risks & mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| USDA/food-safety gate underestimated | Solve co-packer + GFSI **first**; it's the critical path. |
| Can't hit club margin | Validate COGS at scale before pitching; renegotiate co-pack; raise pack size/price only if shelf supports it. |
| Win the order, can't supply it | Confirm co-packer scale + cold-chain capacity **before** accepting club volume. |
| Weak Utah ethnic demand | Position as universal air-fryer chicken snack, not ethnic specialty. |
| Thin margins + slotting/demo costs | Treat Road Shows/demos as paid customer-acquisition; model spoilage & demo cost per warehouse. |
| Freezer-door comprehension | On-pack: plain-English descriptor + "fully cooked" + air-fryer time + appetizing crisp photo. |
