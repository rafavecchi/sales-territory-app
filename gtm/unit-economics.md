## Work backward from the shelf

**Reference SKU: premium 20–24 count bag at $14.99** (~$0.65/piece — defensible vs. $2–4 fresh). A 30-count bag at $11.99 works out to ~$0.40/piece, which is too cheap for a filled, labor-intensive product and collapses the margin.

## Costs to budget *outside* the per-bag margin

- **Slotting / promotional allowances** (grocery more than club).
- **Road Show / demo cost** per warehouse (staff, samples, product) — treat as customer-acquisition cost.
- **Spoilage / returns reserve** (frozen has waste; hold ~2–5%).
- **Insurance** ($2–5M product/general liability — annual premium).
- **Certifications** (GFSI/SQF audit costs — usually the co-packer's, confirm).
- **Working capital** — club pays on terms while you pay the co-packer up front; a won order needs cash to fund the production run. This is the silent killer; model it.

## Sensitivity questions to answer

1. At what **production run size** does COGS drop enough to clear 30%? (Volume is your margin lever.)
2. What's the **all-in demo cost per warehouse**, and how many bags must sell to pay it back?
3. If a warehouse moves **X units/week**, can the co-packer supply it with your lead time — and can you **finance** that run?
4. Does a **dip-included** SKU or a **bigger piece** justify pushing above $14.99, or would you rather hold price and widen margin?

## Pack-size decision

Your stated retail pack is **10–20 count**. For **club**, go **premium: ~20–24 count / ~1.5–2 lb at $14.99** — bigger, quality pieces at ~$0.65 each read as club value while protecting margin. Avoid the 30-count/$11.99 route (~$0.40/piece); it undersells a labor-intensive product and squeezes the economics. Keep the **10–20 ct** pack for regular grocery and independents.

# Co-Packer Quote Cheat Sheet

Use this to set expectations before you call co-packers and to sanity-check what they send back. These are **industry ranges, not quotes** — real numbers swing a lot with meat content, automation, and volume.

## What a coxinha realistically costs to make

Assume a **premium ~1.5–2 lb bag, ~20–24 pieces (~35–40g each)**, fully cooked, breaded, IQF-frozen.

**Built up per finished pound:**

| Component | Startup volume | At scale |
|---|---|---|
| Ingredients (chicken filling + dough + breading) | $1.75–$2.75 | $1.50–$2.25 |
| Co-pack conversion (labor + overhead + their margin) | $2.00–$3.50 | $1.25–$2.00 |
| Packaging (bag + case, allocated) | $0.25–$0.50 | $0.20–$0.35 |
| **Finished COGS / lb** | **~$4.00–$6.50** | **~$3.00–$4.50** |

**Translated to a ~1.5–2 lb bag:**

| Volume tier | COGS / bag |
|---|---|
| Startup / small runs | **~$7–$12** |
| Mid volume | **~$6–$9** |
| High volume / automated | **~$5–$7** |

## The punchline vs. the club math

At **$14.99 retail**, Costco pays you ~$13.15, minus ~$0.90 freight = **~$12.25 to you**.

- At **startup COGS ($7–12/bag)** you range from a healthy ~40% down to roughly break-even — pricing at $14.99 keeps you above water where $11.99/30-ct did not.
- At **~$5–7/bag (scale)** you're at a strong **~40–50%+ margin**.
- **Volume still lowers COGS and widens margin,** but $14.99 means you're no longer betting the whole channel on hitting rock-bottom cost.

## The single biggest cost swing: hand-formed vs. machine-formed

Coxinhas are a *filled, shaped* product — labor-intensive. Many small Brazilian operations **hand-roll** them, which can add **$3–5/lb in labor alone** and won't scale to club volume. At scale you want a co-packer with an **encrusting/forming machine** (Rheon, Unifiller-type) that extrudes a filled teardrop. **Ask every co-packer whether they form by machine or by hand** — it's the difference between a viable club product and one that never pencils.

## Other line items they'll quote (budget separately from per-bag COGS)

- **MOQ / minimum run:** often a full production day — roughly **1,000–5,000 lb** or **500–1,000+ cases** per run. Frequently the real gate for a startup.
- **R&D / first-run / test batch:** **$1,000–$10,000** to dial in recipe + machine settings.
- **Setup / changeover / sanitation per run:** **$500–$2,500**.
- **Custom tooling** (forming-machine die, if needed): **a few thousand $**, one-time.
- **Frozen storage:** **~$15–30/pallet/month** (or ~$0.02–0.05/lb/month).
- **Ingredient markup** (if they source, not you): typically **cost + 10–20%**.

## Levers to bring the quote down

1. **Volume** — biggest lever; get quotes at several run sizes and watch the per-lb drop.
2. **Machine forming**, not hand-rolling.
3. **Chicken thigh** (~$1.50–2.50/lb) instead of breast (~$3–4/lb).
4. **Simplify the filling** — cheaper cream cheese, or skip catupiry.
5. **Right-size the piece** — a slightly smaller piece hits a price point without hurting count.

## Questions to ask every co-packer (for apples-to-apples quotes)

- Do you form **by machine or by hand**? What throughput?
- Are you **USDA-inspected** (poultry/FSIS grant) and **GFSI/SQF certified**? *(both non-negotiable)*
- **MOQ** per run and **minimum annual volume**?
- Quote at **3 volume tiers** (e.g., 2k / 10k / 50k lb) so I can see the curve.
- **R&D, setup, tooling, storage** fees, itemized?
- Do you **source ingredients** or do I supply? What markup?
- **Lead time**, and how fast can you **scale** if a club order lands?
